What is Youth Participatory Action Research (YPAR)?

Young adults act as co-researchers and change agents: they study an issue affecting them and take action on what they learn. Examples below.

Implement this in my community.

How YPAR Works

  1. Choose an issue Youth pick a local problem that affects them.
  2. Collect and analyze data Youth ask their own questions and look at the evidence.
  3. Present findings Youth share results and solutions with people in power.
  4. Take action Youth help carry out changes in their community.

YPAR in Practice

Forsyth County, NC

YouthRISE, Winston-Salem State University

10week summer program
20Black youth in grades 8-12 in the pilot

The program

YouthRISE is a 10-week summer program built on youth participatory action research. Young adults choose a local problem tied to economic mobility, collect and analyze data, and present their findings and proposed solutions to elected officials, business owners, and educators.

The county has very low upward mobility for children from lower-income families. The program aims to build well-being, college and career readiness, and a sense of agency as steps toward changing that.

What the pilot found

Youth took surveys before and after the program. Their scores rose for:

  • Life satisfaction
  • Sense of purpose
  • Educational aspirations (from about a four-year degree to graduate school)
  • Belief that individual action can change the community
  • Desire to contribute to the community

Youth social responsibility did not change significantly.

Keep in mind: The results are promising but preliminary. The sample was small, there was no comparison group, and participants were recruited through referrals.

The program

Youth ADAPT NC is a participatory action research (PAR) project with Hispanic immigrant youth in Charlotte, an emerging immigrant gateway. Fifteen youth signed up and 13 finished. They met for 11 two-hour evening sessions over about three months at the Latin American Coalition, a local advocacy and social services organization.

Their question was how Hispanic youth could improve job access for themselves and their peers. They identified a lack of job-related information as the main barrier: résumés, interviews, navigating college, and finding mentors.

What the youth did

  • Built a website, which drew over 5,000 visitors in its first 18 months.
  • Ran a professional development seminar at a public library, with about 40 attendees, a panel of local employers and workforce leaders, and food from a local restaurant.

How participants felt

In the end-of-project survey, nearly all participants said they enjoyed the project, worked well as a team, and felt their voices were heard. Most said they felt better prepared for future jobs.

Keep in mind: This was a small, self-reported evaluation, so it shows the approach is workable and valued.

Colorado

Learning and Living With Wildfire Smoke

3Colorado high schools
85people enrolled
2027expected primary completion

This University of Washington study, led by Savannah D’Evelyn and funded through the National Institute of Environmental Health Sciences, uses YPAR to reduce air pollution exposure in three Colorado high schools. It began in September 2023 and is active but no longer recruiting.

Part 1: An air quality curriculum

The curriculum is built into existing science-type classes (Food Science, Intro to Agriculture, and Environmental Science Academy), so every student in those classes takes it. It was designed with the Colorado Department of Public Health and Environment and covers:

  • Air quality basics
  • Wildfire smoke
  • Measuring personal exposure
  • How sensors and monitoring networks work
  • Civic action

Part 2: A Student Research Cohort

In each school, 10 to 15 freshmen and sophomores join an after-school cohort. They write their own research questions, such as:

  • How wildfire smoke compares with other pollution sources
  • How outdoor air gets indoors
  • How exposure varies across the building and the school day

They receive indoor, outdoor, and handheld monitors and a manual covering placement, calibration, and data analysis. They then arrange and run a meeting to present their plan to school administrators and facilities staff. New students are recruited each year, with returning students bringing them up to speed.

What the study measures

  • Knowledge and self-efficacy, before and after the curriculum
  • Behavior change in later years: mask use, time outdoors, transportation, and air filtration
  • Actual indoor particulate and carbon dioxide levels before and after the interventions students design, such as filtration changes or anti-idling campaigns

Keep in mind: No results have been posted yet, so this is a model still being tested.

How Will This Benefit Youth?

YPAR benefits youth by empowering them to identify and address issues that affect their lives and communities. It provides a platform for youth to develop critical thinking, leadership, and research skills while contributing to meaningful change (e.g., transform systems and issues closely related to youth).
