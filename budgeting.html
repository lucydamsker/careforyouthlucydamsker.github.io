Participatory Budgeting

Participatory budgeting is a democratic process where community members directly decide how to allocate a portion of public funds.

Implement this in my community.

How Participatory Budgeting Works

  1. Collect ideas Residents submit ideas for projects.
  2. Develop proposals Volunteer delegates work with city staff to turn ideas into proposals.
  3. Vote Community members vote on which proposals to fund.
  4. Implement The city funds and carries out the winning projects.

North Carolina Examples

These are not youth-specific, but they demonstrate the application of participatory budgeting.

Greensboro, NC

Greensboro Participatory Budgeting

$500,000in city funds, evenly divided
5City Council districts
14+age to take part
2 yearsbetween cycles

How it worked

The city’s Department of Budget and Evaluation used a participatory budgeting process to implement voter-approved projects every two years. Residents decided how to spend $500,000 in city funds, evenly divided among five City Council districts.

Any resident over the age of 14 could submit ideas, vote for which ones to fund, and volunteer to develop them into official projects in their neighborhood. Funding was restricted to capital projects.

Past approved projects

  • Crosswalks
  • Bike racks
  • Bus shelters
  • Park upgrades, like play equipment and picnic tables
  • Neighborhood murals

Keep in mind: The program was suspended by the City of Greensboro in July 2023.

Durham, NC

Participatory Budgeting Durham

2018year launched
$8.5M+invested across four cycles
$2.4–2.7Mtypically set aside per cycle
13+age to take part
20%of Cycle IV ideas came from students

Participatory Budgeting Durham is a citywide democratic process that lets residents and students directly decide how to spend a portion of the city’s capital budget. It operates on a two-year cycle, and the City Council typically sets aside $2.4 million to $2.7 million per cycle.

Who can take part

Anyone who lives or attends school in Durham and is 13 years or older can participate. That makes youth a central part of the process, alongside multilingual outreach and partnerships that include voting access at the Durham County Jail.

What it has funded

Across its four cycles, PB Durham has funded a wide range of neighborhood upgrades:

  • Park renovations
  • Bus shelters
  • Public art murals
  • Public charging stations
  • A downtown public restroom

Four main stages

Each two-year cycle is overseen by a resident-led Steering Committee and moves through:

  • Idea collection from the public
  • Proposal development by volunteer Budget Delegates working with city staff
  • A citywide public vote
  • Final implementation

How it has changed

Early cycles awarded grants to community organizations like Book Harvest and H.E.A.R.T.S. Recent cycles focus strictly on one-time capital and infrastructure improvements that do not require new city staffing.

Youth-Specific Models From Other States

New York City

It’s Our Money

9–24age range to submit ideas and vote
$100,000total budget
5winning projects
$20,000maximum per winning project

It’s Our Money is a youth-led democratic cycle run by the New York City Civic Engagement Commission in partnership with the Coro New York Leadership Center.

How it works

  • Idea collection. New York City residents ages 9 to 24 brainstorm and submit proposals for community projects that address youth needs.
  • Proposal development. Youth leaders and fellows evaluate the submissions and turn feasible ideas into concrete project proposals for a final ballot.
  • Vote. Young New Yorkers within that same 9-to-24 age range vote directly on which proposals they want to see funded.
  • Funding. The five projects receiving the most votes are awarded up to $20,000 each from the $100,000 budget, and local community-based organizations are contracted to bring the winning youth initiatives to life.
Bloomington, Indiana

Bloomington Youth Participatory Budget

$15,000set aside by the city
7–12grades that can take part
5final ballot proposals
2019first approved budget cycle

The Bloomington Youth Participatory Budget (YPB) is a municipal initiative in which the city sets aside $15,000 for local middle and high school students to allocate toward community projects. It is facilitated by the City of Bloomington Commission on the Status of Children and Youth.

How it works

Monroe County students in grades 7 through 12 can submit ideas and vote on public improvements costing $15,000 or less, across categories like arts, environment, and recreation.

Guided by a youth-led Steering Committee and student delegates, participants collect peer ideas, research feasibility with city staff, and narrow submissions down to five final ballot proposals.

Results

First approved for the 2019 budget cycle, the pilot program saw youth voters select The Moss Project, an air-filtering public art installation. The city renewed the $15,000 allocation in 2022 to continue engaging young residents in local governance.

Keep in mind: This program does not appear to be currently active. Public city records and commission updates show no ongoing cycles, active funding, or recent announcements since the 2022 budget cycle.

How Will This Benefit Youth?

According to Augsberger, Gecker, and Collins (2018), PB empowers youth by giving them a real sense of control over actual money, building their skills and confidence through training and peer mentoring, connecting them with supportive adults, and letting them take concrete action.
