Implementing YPAR in my community

How to start a youth participatory action research program in your community.

Download the YPAR guide

1. Pick a focus and a partner

  • Choose an issue your community already feels (job access, economic mobility, smoke, heat, water).
  • Partner with a trusted organization that has a youth-friendly space; put the arrangement in writing.
  • Bring in a university, health agency, or similar group for curriculum, facilitation, and evaluation.

2. Start small, recruit through trusted people

  • Aim for 10-15 youth: big enough for ideas, small enough to build friendships.
  • Recruit through schools, teachers, youth services, and peer referrals; get parent consent for minors.
  • In schools, build lessons into existing classes, then add an after-school research team.

3. Remove barriers to showing up

  • Meet within walking distance or near transit; provide bus passes or carpools.
  • Serve food at every session, ideally from local businesses.
  • Offer a stipend or gift card, or non-monetary value like references and credit. Order supplies early.
  • Reach youth on the channels they use (group texts, apps) and follow up individually.

4. Build trust and structure first

  • Open with icebreakers, group-written ground rules, and a clear explanation of what happens with their work.
  • Share an agenda each session and reflect at the end; check in on how people are feeling.
  • Mix pair, small-group, and whole-group work so quieter youth are heard.

5. Give youth real tools and real skills

  • Let youth choose the question and the product; adults supply structure and logistics.
  • Provide the materials and manuals they need (sensors, survey tools, web design).
  • Add guest speakers, leadership activities, networking events, and media opportunities.

6. Make sure someone with power listens

  • End with a public presentation to officials, employers, educators, or school leaders.
  • Secure commitments in advance that decision-makers will respond.
  • Design a win-win-win: youth gain skills, local businesses gain customers, government gains value.

7. Measure and protect

  • Use simple pre/post surveys; add a comparison group if possible.
  • Track real-world results where you can, such as indoor air quality.
  • Plan consent and confidentiality early; let youth decide whether to share names or images publicly.

8. Plan for the long term

  • Celebrate accomplishments with certificates and recognition.
  • Decide from the start who keeps the work going after funding ends.
  • Scale in phases: pilot, add sites and build capacity, then institutionalize (for example, in schools).
