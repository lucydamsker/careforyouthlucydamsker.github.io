Photovoice Implementation Guide

A brief overview of how to run a photovoice project with youth in your community. The full handout has checklists, tips, and a sample timeline.

Download the Photovoice guide

Before You Start

Plan these basics with your team, and with youth, before the first session.

Purpose and group

Choose a topic and one to three guiding questions, and recruit a group small enough for real discussion.

Time and materials

Plan several sessions plus time to take photos. Youth can use their own phones, and you will need printing, food, and a venue.

Consent and support

Gather youth assent and guardian consent, prepare photo releases, and have local support resources ready.

The Eight Steps

  • 1. Set the purpose with youth. Choose the topic and questions together.
  • 2. Recruit and build trust. Explain the project and check in on consent often.
  • 3. Train. Cover camera basics and photo ethics, with practice.
  • 4. Take photos. Give youth one to two weeks to photograph their experiences, needs, and hopes.
  • 5. Select and discuss. Youth choose photos, write captions, and talk about them using SHOWeD.
  • 6. Find themes together. Sort printed photos into themes in small groups.
  • 7. Share and take action. Hold an exhibit or presentation for decision makers.
  • 8. Reflect and follow up. Thank youth, share outcomes, and follow up on commitments.

Discussion Questions: SHOWeD

Use these five questions for each photo, in a group or one-on-one.

  • S What do you See here?
  • H What is really Happening here?
  • O How does this relate to Our lives?
  • e Why does this problem or strength Exist?
  • D What can we Do about it?

Ethics and Keeping Youth in the Lead

Ethics and safety

  • Informed consent, with the option to withdraw at any time
  • Photo releases for anyone recognizable
  • Youth choose how they are credited
  • Never photograph risky situations
  • Be honest that photos can identify people

Questions to ask at every step

  • Did youth help choose the topic and questions?
  • Who chose the photos and captions?
  • Did youth check the themes?
  • Who decides how the work is shown and credited?
